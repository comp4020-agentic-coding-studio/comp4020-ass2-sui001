Edward Hall's proxemics splits interpersonal distance into four zones —
intimate, personal, social and public — and the zone people default to
says more about the relationship than either person would say out loud.
Watching people unconsciously negotiate these distances is one of the
fastest ways to read a room's actual social structure.

## Before the session

In the same space you've been using for your diagnosis (or a similar one),
measure or estimate the distances people keep from each other across at
least three different relationship types — strangers, colleagues, close
friends or family. Note which zone each pairing sits in.

## In the session

We map the results against Hall's zones and talk through the diagnosis
assessment, due this week: what makes a diagnosis specific rather than a
restatement of "personal space is a thing."
