A public third space — a park, a library, a café with no purchase
required to sit — is meant to belong to everyone equally. Whether it
actually does depends on small, constant acts of noticing: making way,
sharing a bench, not treating the space as a personal office because your
laptop is open.

## Before the session

Pick a public third space and observe it for at least 20 minutes with your
own phone away, not just silenced. Watch for who yields to whom, who
claims more space than their share, and what happens when someone
new arrives.

## In the session

We put this week's observations next to Week 3's civil inattention and
Week 2's third-place criteria: does this space actually function as
everyone's, or as a corridor a few people have quietly claimed?

## Afterwards

Bring this space to mind in Week 10, when we look at how much of what
you've just described is culturally specific rather than universal.
