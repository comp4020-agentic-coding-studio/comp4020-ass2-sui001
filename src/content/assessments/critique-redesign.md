## The brief

> Find one shared space that currently gets coexistence wrong, and redesign
> it so it doesn't.

By now you've diagnosed a space and tested a protocol on a group you
belong to. This is where you turn that method outward: pick a space
(could be the one from your diagnosis, or somewhere new) where the
physical layout, the stated rules, or the process people are expected to
follow actively works against good coexistence. A common room with no
sightlines into it. A studio where the loud machines and the quiet desks
share a wall. An office with a "flexible seating" policy and nowhere to
leave your things.

Critique it properly first: name what's failing and why, using at least
two frameworks from the semester. Then redesign it, not a vague
aspiration that people be nicer, but a specific change to the layout, the
stated norms, or the process that runs the space, that you can argue would
actually change the outcome.

## What you submit

A single document, readable without you in the room, that:

- describes the space and its current failure mode in enough detail that
  the problem is obvious once named
- diagnoses that failure using the course's frameworks
- proposes a concrete redesign (a floor plan change, a new stated
  protocol, a changed process) and explains why it would work where the
  current arrangement doesn't
- is honest about the redesign's own limits: no proposal fixes everything,
  and knowing what it won't fix is part of the mark
