# Policies and support

## Extensions

Field work runs on real weeks with real people in them — illness, care
responsibilities and shift work all get in the way of a scheduled
observation. Email your convenor before the due date with what happened and
how much time you need; a short, late extension request is fine, a long
explanation is not required. Extensions are not available once an assessment
has been marked and returned.

## Academic integrity, applied to this course

Diagnosis, protocol and redesign work must describe spaces and interactions
you actually observed. You may discuss your observations with classmates —
comparing notes on how a shared kitchen behaves is part of the method — but
the write-up, the protocol and the redesign must be your own account and
your own design. Fabricating an observation is treated the same as
fabricating data anywhere else: as a breach, not a shortcut.

## A note on privacy

You will be writing about real rooms and real people who did not sign up to
be studied. Describe behaviour, not identities: no names, no photographs,
no details that would let a classmate or a marker identify a specific
person you observed without their knowledge. If in doubt, generalise the
detail until it is no longer traceable to one person.

## Getting help

If something about a week's task is unclear, ask before the due date rather
than guessing — a five-minute clarifying email is always cheaper than a
redone field observation. If you are finding the personal or emotional
content of a week difficult rather than merely unclear, say so; the course
covers real friction between real people, and it is fine to ask for a
different observation site if the assigned one is not workable for you.
