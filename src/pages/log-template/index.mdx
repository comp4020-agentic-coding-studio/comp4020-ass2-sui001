# How to log a field observation

Most of what you submit this semester comes out of a log you kept in the
moment, not something reconstructed the night before it's due. A log that
just says "everyone was pretty considerate" is a log about nothing; the
frameworks on the [readings page](/readings/) need something more specific
to work on than that. Use this template whenever a Tute or assessment asks
you to log something.

## The template

| Field | What goes here |
| --- | --- |
| When | The actual date and time, not "sometime in the afternoon"; you'll need to sequence entries against each other later |
| Where | The specific space, and what kind of space it is (home, work, studio, third place, public) |
| Who | How many people, and their apparent relationship to each other and to you (stranger, roommate, colleague, regular) |
| What happened | The observable behaviour only, not your interpretation yet |
| Framework | Which framework it looks like an instance of, if any; leave it blank rather than force one |
| Your read | One line: what you think it shows, now that it's separated from the raw behaviour |

## A worked example

| Field | Example |
| --- | --- |
| When | 2027-03-03, 8:12am |
| Where | Local café (third place), regular morning crowd |
| Who | Two strangers, both solo, adjacent tables |
| What happened | Both reached for the shared sugar caddy at once, one paused half a second, the other took it first, neither made eye contact |
| Framework | Civil inattention |
| Your read | The pause was the coordination, resolved before it needed a word |

## What doesn't count as a log entry

"People were nice," "nothing much happened," "the vibe was chill": these
are conclusions with the evidence thrown away. If you can't reconstruct the
incident from your own note a week later, it isn't logged, it's vibes.

Keep the raw log separate from your analysis. Fill in "What happened" the
moment it happens; "Framework" and "Your read" can wait until you're
somewhere you can think.
