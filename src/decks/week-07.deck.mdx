# Workplace

You didn't pick your desk-mates. You're paid to coexist with them anyway.

---

## A narrower kind of coexistence

- home and third places run on choice; a workplace mostly doesn't
- "professional" is doing the coexistence work anyway, on a schedule
  someone else set
- this is the one context in the course where quitting isn't always a
  live option, which is exactly why the skill matters here

---

{/* _class: quote */}

> It doesn't matter who gets the credit, as long as the task gets done.

---

## Testing that line against real cases

- holds up: a team ships something, and arguing over whose idea it was
  would only slow the next one down
- breaks down: someone consistently takes credit for work that isn't
  theirs, and nothing ever corrects it
- the difference isn't the line; it's whether the system around it is
  actually fair over time

---

## Open-plan offices, revisited

Week 3's civil inattention and Week 4's proxemics zones, applied to a
room you didn't choose:

- who yields the aisle, and does it correlate with seniority
- whose desk becomes the default meeting spot, and who was never asked
- what "flexible seating" actually redistributes, and to whom
